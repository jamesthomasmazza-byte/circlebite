Reading labels is a second job.

Recipes change without notice. Allergens hide under other names: whey and casein are milk, semolina is wheat, tahini is sesame. The "may contain" line is easy to miss in a busy aisle.

And you are not the only one buying food. Every grandparent, babysitter and coach has to know the same list and read the same small print.

One profile, everyone who feeds your child.

You own your child's profile and decide who joins. A co-parent can help manage it. Grandparents and sitters can follow it and scan with it. When someone in the circle reports an allergen on a label, the circle sees the warning, and a follower sees their role, never their name.

It shows its work, so you can judge it.

Every result gives its reason

You see whether a warning came from the ingredients, the product's allergen statement, or another parent's report. When the data can't answer, it says "Unable to confirm" instead of guessing.

Reports need a photo of the label

A parent who spots an allergen the product data missed reports it with a photo of the package, so the evidence comes with the warning.

Warnings are easy to raise, hard to remove

It takes two separate families to confirm a warning for everyone. Taking a warning away is never automatic: a report that an allergen isn't there waits for review, so one mistaken report can't remove it.

A second look, not a replacement

CircleBite is a screening aid. Product data can be wrong or out of date, so always check the physical label, especially for "may contain" warnings.
